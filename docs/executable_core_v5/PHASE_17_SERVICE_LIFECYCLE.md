# Phase 17AO: prepaid service lifetimes and a resident broker

## Baseline and scope

AO is additive from completed AN evidence
`db7f9e622e14d7466319c5e21b41a20c768e7a12`. The inherited controller,
objective, evaluator, durable worker reservations, mutual TLS, protected broker
semantics, immutable root constitution, and v5 roadmap remain unchanged.

AN's accounting inventory is deliberately a coverage declaration, not a mutable
service budget. Its actual nonrefundable budget owner covers the declared AL
reference evaluator workload. The AN broker executes in the trusted capture
parent. AO adds a durable owner for a bounded **service lifetime** and places the
credential-bearing broker dispatch in a separate resident process.

The two narrow AO results are `prepaid_service_lifetime_checked` and
`resident_broker_service_checked`. They require the original native records,
source bindings, generated Lean certificates, exact designated rejections, and
completed pinned-compiler lineage. They do not establish independently
commissioned production deployment, physical currentness, clone exclusion,
complete campaign accounting, immutable-root acceptance, or promotion.

## Prepayment before native startup

The trusted supervisor holds an exclusive local ledger ownership lock. A fixed
commission binds the actual source closure, campaign commission, declared
principal scope, retained reference anchor, lifetime envelope, and total budget.
The expected commission identity is supplied outside the frontend protocol.

A fresh lifetime identifier consumes the complete fixed envelope permanently.
The ledger commits that reservation before either the authority or resident
broker process starts. A separate launch consumption is durable before process
creation. Startup failure does not refund the envelope. The resident process
also retains a non-reusable launch marker in the protected broker state directory;
its configuration cannot be replayed to acquire that same local lifetime again.

The six coordinates have different units and are compared componentwise:

| Coordinate | Declared unit and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| `service_lifetimes` | One admitted service lifetime, including its bounded setup and shutdown protocol |
| `process_launch_attempts` | Counted authority, resident broker, frontend and selected worker process attempts |
| `frontend_input_attempts` | Resident input frames, including malformed and refused input |
| `request_bytes` | Bytes consumed from the resident service's frontend input pipe, including delimiters |
| `response_bytes` | Bytes emitted on resident stdout, including ready/reply/stop frames and delimiters |
| `authority_tls_connections` | Connection attempts admitted by the inherited finite authority lifetime profile |

These counts are not CPU time, memory, energy, total storage, total network bytes,
or a currency. They are not added together. They are not converted into AL's
evaluator accounting units. A fixed, prepaid operational envelope avoids allowing
unbounded rejected-input work merely because no evaluator grant was issued.
It does not supply a physical upper cost for every operation within the envelope.
Commissioning, admission machinery, formal checking, evidence processing,
provisioning and historical work still need justified physical accounting.

The source-bound resident parser limits frame length and aggregate input/output
bytes. It consumes the attempt and input-byte allowance before JSON parsing or
broker dispatch. A malformed frame therefore leaves a retained charged record.
An oversized or truncated frame terminates the lifetime conservatively; the
service does not drain an unbounded stream to recover framing. A finite trusted
slot inventory bounds this reference service's lifetime. It is not a general
remote service endpoint or an autonomous task scheduler.

The inherited AM authority retains its finite TLS connection limit. Restarting
that authority is admitted only inside a newly prepaid lifetime in the selected
supervisor path. The new service ledger does not replace AL's exact evaluator
grant reservation or launch-consumption transition.

## Ownership, restart and ambiguity

Ledger operations require the complete expected cursor and current owner. Local
serialization, exact retained-prefix comparison, canonical records, readback and
append-only logical transitions support the selected operational history under
the existing filesystem, kernel and trusted-supervisor assumptions.

Closing a completed or failed lifetime preserves its entire reservation.
Reopening a completed ledger advances ownership and epoch; it retains cumulative
charges and consumed identifiers. An unresolved reservation or consumed launch
blocks automatic reopening. The implementation does not infer that a missing
process handle authorizes takeover, replacement, refund, or a second worker.

The selected campaign starts two resident broker processes across two authority
lifetimes. The first executes the inherited AN actions, including one abandoned
reply and a blocked mutation retry. The second reconciles against authenticated
history without reissuing that worker, then observes the inherited aggregate
evaluator denial. A third service reservation is refused before process launch
because the service lifetime budget is exhausted. A separate interrupted
reservation retains its full charge and refuses automatic reopening.

The resident owns each credential-bearing AM client and the unchanged AN broker
dispatch. Native client PIDs are joined to the resident process, while actual
frontend PIDs remain joined to their original trusted launcher and namespace
observations. Original AL grants, challenges, worker process records and
completion evidence remain the authority for the two new reference sessions.

## Deployment evidence and the external commissioning boundary

The local reference provisions its own test credentials and public commission
records. Its trusted supervisor and resident process run under the available
host identity. The Linux frontend and actual evaluator workers retain AN's
namespace restrictions and protected-path observations. A namespace UID is not
an independently administered host account. On Windows this increment validates
the bounded service and broker protocol only; it supplies no Linux namespace or
independent Windows service-token credit.

AO includes a deployment plan and read-only inspector to make missing external
inputs concrete. A viable production installation must bind all of the following:

1. An independently retained commissioning identity and expected plan digest.
2. The actual host-scoped service, controller and candidate/worker principals.
3. Administrator-controlled source, interpreter, runtime dependencies and their
   parent directories. A privileged service cannot safely execute a runtime or
   checkout that the controller can replace.
4. Authority state, service budget, private credentials, broker pending state and
   retained anchors protected from both actual untrusted principals.
5. Live process identity and access-denial evidence from the actual controller
   and actual worker, including denied writes to protected parent directories.
6. A separately justified current-head and fencing backend outside the rollback
   and clone domain. Each consequential effect must be covered by its ownership
   argument, rather than relying only on a startup declaration.
7. A complete accounting interpretation and evidence for every required campaign
   category, including ambiguous and unsuccessful work.
8. An actual immutable-root acceptance result for a promotion-capable campaign.

The inspector does not create accounts, elevate privileges, install services,
change permissions, configure a remote host, or accept self-declared production
success. Missing or unsupported observations leave deployment acceptance false.
The portable handoff is not an administrator commissioning transaction.

### What local exclusivity means

Within one authoritative local ledger and protected launch-marker store, stale
cursors, reused lifetime identifiers and repeated local launch consumption are
refused. Copying that complete state to a second writable domain copies the
ability to reproduce its transitions. A local lock, signed record or another
database in the same rollback domain does not exclude that copy. The Lean model
retains this clone counterexample. Exactly-once physical work after arbitrary
crashes and rollback is not inferred from a successful local state transition.

## Evidence and formal interpretation

`service_lifecycle_capture.py` retains original service, authority, frontend,
worker, ledger and deployment-inspection bytes. Its verifier reconstructs the
ledger, joins each actual service configuration to its prepaid grant and launch
consumption, recomputes bounded counters from original streams, and retains the
exact inherited broker/TLS/worker joins. A summary or supplied projection cannot
stand in for these records.

`ServiceLifecycle.lean` models fixed commissioning, vector reservation, owner and
cursor checks, once-only launch consumption, bounded charges, closeout without
refund, restart preservation and explicit denial. Selected declarations are
generated from the independently checked original ledger events. Named axiom
audits and exact designated rejection checks use the unchanged pinned stage
runner. The model does not introduce physical-trust axioms or construct a full
`LearnedAcceptedStep` or `AutonomousAcceptedStep`.

Saved-evidence verification reopens retained bytes and completed formal-stage
records. It does not rerun historical campaigns, issue new workers, or silently
recompile missing native objects. Physical roots and private credentials remain
outside public Git evidence. The complete execution recovery ZIP includes the
selected compiled-object copies and original gate invocations, but excludes
private keys; the full physical workspace must also be retained separately.
